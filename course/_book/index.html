Factory Physics Through Factorio

An experiential, reproducible laboratory for production-system behavior

Author

FISL

Welcome

This is the course text for Factory Physics Through Factorio: a sequence of laboratory experiments in which you operate, measure, and redesign real (simulated) production systems, and meet the formal theory only after you have experienced the phenomenon it explains.

The laboratory is FISL — the Factorio Industrial Systems Laboratory. Factorio provides the physics of production: machines, belts, buffers, recipes, power. FISL provides the experimental apparatus around it: controlled material supply, declared system boundaries, an exact experiment clock, and measurement whose every number carries its method, window, and validity.

The governing idea:

Use Factorio to model the system. Use FISL to model the experiment.

How the labs work

Each lab follows the same protocol:

  1. Encounter a deliberately constructed production system.
  2. Run a controlled, timed experiment while it operates (or while you modify it).
  3. Read the report — reproducible measurements, not impressions.
  4. Meet the theory that names what you observed.
  5. Intervene and re-run, then compare attempts side by side.

Every run you perform produces an auditable record; two runs of the same scenario are exactly comparable, and the deterministic simulation means a repeated run without intervention reproduces its numbers exactly. Your experiments are experiments in the scientific sense.

Between labs, short interludes develop the theory the phenomena have earned: each one builds only on what earlier labs made you measure, and each ends with claims stated precisely enough to be wrong — which the next lab then tests. Theory here is never an announcement; it is a hypothesis you are about to check against a system you can walk around in.

What this course assumes

No Factorio experience is required — but that promise is kept by the Before the First Lab chapter, not by wishful thinking: it teaches the handful of controls the course uses (there are about a dozen), the plant-floor reading of the game’s objects, and the one-time setup. Start there if you’ve never played. No mathematics beyond arithmetic is required to do the labs; the interludes introduce each formal relationship as it is earned.

What the simulation assumes away

Factorio’s machines execute perfectly, never fail, and hide nothing. Real factories — and real organizations — do none of these things. Each lab ends by stating explicitly what the simulation represented, what it omitted, and which conclusions are (and are not) warranted. Treat that discipline as part of the subject: knowing the limits of a model is what makes it usable.
