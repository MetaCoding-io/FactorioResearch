The Practice Range

Scenario: fp-sandbox · self-paced, then a 1-minute inspection pass

Six manipulations cover every build the seven labs will ask of your hands. This scenario exists so you rehearse them where nothing is at stake: Lab 0’s little one-machine world, a toolbox stocked with every part you’ll need, and a report at the end that tells you — honestly, the FISL way — which drills the world actually shows.

Skip this chapter freely if you build in Factorio already. The drills take a newcomer perhaps twenty minutes; Lab 3 is more fun when the chest splice isn’t your first.

How a practice session works

fisl run scenarios/factory-physics/fp-sandbox

Connect your client as usual. Everything happens on the READY floor — unmeasured, unhurried, and reset by the next fisl run. Build the drills below at your own pace. When you’re done, press Start Experiment: the one-minute pass that follows is your inspection — when it ends, the controller reads the world and grades the drills, and fisl report runs/<run_id> shows a Drill card (“5/6 verified”) next to a real, tiny measurement of the line you may have just rebuilt.

Run it as many times as you like. Aborting costs nothing.

The six drills

Every control named here is in the Before the First Lab table.

  1. d1_place_belts — lay a belt. Take transport belts from the toolbox chest and place a short run of at least four tiles anywhere in the zone (south of the line is open ground).
  2. d2_rotate_belt — turn it. At least one of your belts should face north or south (R rotates the item in your hand before placing — or the placed belt under your cursor). The original line runs east; the check looks for any belt that doesn’t.
  3. d3_chest_splice — buffer the flow. The move Labs 4 and 6 call “splice a chest into the belt”: remove one belt tile (yours or the line’s), place a wooden chest in the gap, and set an inserter on each side — one feeding the chest, one taking from it, arrows pointing along the flow.
  4. d4_machine_swap — upgrade a machine. Place an assembling-machine-2 (swap it for the line’s machine, or place it beside the line) and set its recipe to machine workpiece. This is Lab 4’s solution B and Lab 6’s winning move, rehearsed.
  5. d5_enable_condition — gate an inserter. Drag a red or green wire (Alt+R / Alt+G) from any chest or belt to any inserter, open the inserter, and set an enable condition (for example: rough workpiece < 2). This is the pull gate of Lab 3, rehearsed. Remember the 9-tile wire reach.
  6. d6_combinator_pair — wire two combinators. Place a constant combinator and a decider combinator and wire them together. Set any signal on the constant and any condition on the decider — the check only asks that they share a network. (Wire the decider’s output to a lamp if you want to see it do something.) Lab 5’s admission clock is this pair, grown up.

Reading your first report

The Drill card is the last section of the report, and it is deliberately not a measurement: drills grade practice and touch no metric. Above it you’ll find the real thing — average WIP, throughput, the machine’s productive fraction — measured over your one-minute pass with the same honesty rules every lab uses. If you swapped the machine in drill 4, look at the per-machine table: the old machine’s eligibility ends where the new one’s begins. You’ve already seen dynamic membership work, three labs before it’s taught.

A drill marked not yet names what the check looked for and didn’t find. Fix it and run again — the range resets, your hands remember.
