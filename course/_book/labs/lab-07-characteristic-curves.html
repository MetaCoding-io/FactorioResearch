14  Lab 7 — The Characteristic Curves

Scenario: fp-07-characteristic-curves · five runs of ~12 minutes (the sweep) · runnable any time after Lab 3

Hands you’ll need

Nothing, to run the reference sweep — the gates are scripted. To add your own points (the assignment), the full circuit kit: Practice Range drills d5_enable_condition and d6_combinator_pair.

The Accounting of Flow interlude committed to two formulas and drew them as a picture: THbest(w)=min(w/T0,rb)\mathrm{TH}_{\text{best}}(w) = \min(w/T_0,\, r_b) and CTbest(w)=max(T0,w/rb)\mathrm{CT}_{\text{best}}(w) = \max(T_0,\, w/r_b), with the knee at the critical WIP W0=rbT0W_0 = r_b T_0. Labs 3 through 6 visited single points on those curves. This lab buys the whole curve: the same line as Lab 3, a WIP cap you can set to any level, and one run per level. At the end you will have drawn the signature figure of Factory Physics from your own measured data — and found the knee.

14.1 The instrument: a ledger made of wire

Capping system WIP requires knowing system WIP, live, in-game. The gate does it the only honest way — the same way FISL does:

  1. a tap belt just inside the source reads admissions as pulses;
  2. a tap belt just before the sink reads completions as pulses;
  3. two arithmetic combinators sign them (+1, −1) onto one signal W;
  4. a self-looped decider accumulates the difference — a memory cell whose running value is admissions − completions = WIP;
  5. the source inserter is enabled only while W < w.

You are rebuilding Lab 0’s conservation ledger out of combinators, and the run’s census will agree with your wire — the rare lab where the learner’s contraption and the laboratory’s instrument are the same object. (The completion pulses travel home along the line’s own belts as a relay chain, read-disabled, every hop checked: the 9-tile wire-reach lesson from Lab 3, institutionalized.) This CONWIP mechanism — a constant WIP cap enforced at admission — is the control policy Hopp & Spearman’s ch. 10 builds its case around; here it is five entities.

Then plot the measured points: TH against measured average WIP and CT against measured average WIP. Two honesty rules make the plot meaningful:

  • The x-coordinate is the measured average WIP, never the nominal cap — the gate’s timing slack and belt discreteness make them differ, and the report’s number is the true one.
  • Expect measured points at or below the best-case TH curve (and at or above the CT curve). The formulas are bounds; belts and inserter swings are real mechanisms. The gap between your points and the bound is itself data — where does it come from?

14.3 The assignment

The reference sweep leaves gaps — nothing between 14 and 24, nothing between 24 and the baseline’s ~52. Pick a cap we didn’t run, build your own gate (the toolbox holds a full counter kit; the solution scripts are readable blueprints), and add your point to the plot. Two questions to answer with your additions:

  1. Where exactly is the knee? Bracket W0W_0 from measurement alone, then compare with rbT0r_b T_0 computed from a stopwatch walk of the empty line. Lab 3’s pull run (WIP 19.16 at full throughput) already told you the knee is at or below 19 — sharpen it.
  2. What does the flat region cost? Every point right of the knee has the same throughput; read the CT column as the price tag of the inventory. Little’s Law says the flat region’s CT must be exactly w/rbw/r_b — check it.

14.4 Debrief

  • The machine-state fractions tell the low-WIP story: at w=4w = 4, which machines are starved and how much? The constraint’s idle time left of the knee is the only throughput cost in this whole deterministic world — name the mechanism.
  • Your gate held WIP constant; Lab 3’s gate held one buffer constant. Both landed on the flat region. What does CONWIP’s system-level cap give you that the local gate didn’t? (Hint: what happens to each if a middle machine slows down for a minute?)
  • The curves you drew are the deterministic best case. Hopp & Spearman’s ch. 7 draws two more on the same axes: the worst case and the practical worst case. Both need variability to mean anything — which is precisely where this course’s sequel begins. Your knee at W0W_0 is the last thing determinism can teach: with variability, operating at the knee starves the bottleneck whenever anything wobbles, and the whole art becomes how far right of it to sit.
